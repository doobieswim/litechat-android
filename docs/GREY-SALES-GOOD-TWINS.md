## Say it like I'm five

Old bad salesmen were good at **one** thing: they knew which button in a person’s head makes them say yes before they think.

The button is not evil.  
The **lie**, the **clock**, and the **trap door** are evil.

We keep the button.  
We throw away the lie, the fake clock, and the trap door.

One sentence: **Make the yes easy and true. Never make the no hard or shameful.**

## The rule for “use it in a good way”

A tactic is allowed here only if **all four** are true:

1. The fact is real (size, price, memory, what the app does).
2. The person can walk away in one tap with no shame.
3. We would still be proud if they read this file.
4. It does not break Play policy, FTC rules, or `THEME-SHOW-DONT-TELL.md`.

If it needs a fake countdown, a hidden fee, a “don’t you love your family” line, or a hard cancel — it is **out**.

## 1. Snake oil / patent medicine (1905)

Samuel Hopkins Adams wrote *The Great American Fraud* in *Collier’s*. America was swallowing alcohol, opium, and “undiluted fraud.” A majority of sick people get better anyway — the bottle took the credit. Fake or grateful testimonials did the rest. Scare ads rode real epidemics (yellow fever, meningitis). The 1906 Food and Drug Act followed.[1][2]

**The button:** fear + hope + a story of a saved neighbor.

**The trap:** the bottle did not do what it said. Ingredients were hidden. “Cures 37 things.”

**Honest twin for us:**

- One job: chat with *their* key. We do not think. We do not cure.
- If the phone is low on memory, a **plain** note. That fear is real. We do not invent a plague.
- No “runs any model / 7B on 4GB.” That is SoftRAM in a new bottle.

**Never:** fake testimonials, cure-all listing, hiding what is in the APK.

## 2. Door-to-door dump-the-dirt (Kirby and cousins)

For 100+ years Kirby sold by walking into the house and *running the machine on their floor*.[9] The demo was the close. The grey part was staying until you signed, a $2,000 commission price, and “you’d be stupid to say no.”

**The button:** I saw it work *here*, on *my* dirt.

**The trap:** you cannot leave your own kitchen. Price appears late. Insult if you hesitate.

**Honest twin:**

- First message on **their** phone, **their** key, in under a minute. That is the dump-the-dirt.
- They can close the app. We do not follow them.
- Price of Pro is visible before they tap Pay. $4.99 once. No “let me talk to my manager.”

**Never:** stay-until-you-buy flows. Insults. Surprise price after the demo.

## 3. Boiler room (stocks)

A cheap room. Scripts. Only good news. “Buy now or miss it.” Fancy titles. House stock the firm already owned. Pump, then dump. The SEC still warns: pressure to buy quickly, unsolicited offers, “high return / no risk.” Utah describes three contacts: make friends → hype the miracle → slam the fake deadline. That last step is the crime.[6][7][8]

**The button:** greed + belonging + fear of missing a secret.

**The trap:** the stock is junk or fake. You are not allowed to think.

**I will not write their script.**

**Honest twin of each step (not a call center):**

| Their step | We never do | Honest twin |
|---|---|---|
| Cold call | Unsolicited “invest in us” spam | They opened the store or the APK |
| Fancy title | Pretend we *are* OpenAI | “You bring the key. We are the small window.” |
| Only good news | Hide the red memory cell | Show the red cell |
| Fake deadline | “Offer ends in 3:00” | No clock unless a real Play sale exists |
| Guaranteed riches | “Save $240/year!!!” as a stunt | Quiet: no monthly bill. Their API bill is theirs |
| Can’t sell / hard exit | Trap Pro | One purchase, keep it, or don’t buy |

**Never:** cold calls, guaranteed returns, house-stock hype, insulting the person who waits.

## 4. Negative option / “you forgot to cancel”

Columbia House and today’s “free trial → surprise club.” Silence is treated as yes. Cancel is a maze. The FTC’s 2024 update says: tell the whole deal first, get a real yes, make cancel as easy as sign-up (“Click to Cancel”).[5]

**The button:** laziness + sunk time. People do not fight a maze.

**The trap:** they are billed because they failed a test.

**Honest twin (we already planned this):**

- Free chat is really free (one small banner).
- Pro is **pay once**. No club. No “you forgot.”
- If we ever add a recurring thing, cancel is one tap on the same screen they used to buy. Until then: **do not add a subscription.**

**Never:** silent enroll, hard cancel, “trial” that hides the date it starts charging.

## 5. Dark patterns (named 2010, old as dirt)

Harry Brignull named them so we could point. Types that matter here: fake urgency, fake scarcity, fake social proof, confirmshaming, hidden costs, hidden subscription, nagging, preselection, sneak, trick wording, hard to cancel, bait-and-switch.[3][4]

**The button:** habit, shame, and a brain that hates losing a deal.

**The trap:** the screen lies about time, stock, popularity, or the meaning of the button.

**Honest twin of each (everyday words):**

| Dark pattern | Honest twin |
|---|---|
| Fake “3 left” | Real: “This phone is low on memory.” |
| Fake countdown | None. Or a real dated Play promo if we ever run one |
| Fake “2,481 people bought” | No number until it is true |
| Confirmshame (“No thanks, I hate saving”) | “Not now” is as calm as “Pay once” |
| Hidden $ after checkout | $4.99 and Play’s cut **before** Pay |
| Banner that looks like Send | Banner looks like a banner |
| Pre-checked “also charge me monthly” | Default is free chat |
| Must make an account to try | No account |
| Nag every send | One banner. Not a pop-up on every message |
| “Free” TurboTax bait | Free chat works. Pro is extra and labeled |
| Trick double-negative | Buttons say what they do |

## 6. Classic closes (the grey ones)

Sales classes still teach these. Some are just manners. Some are traps.

| Close | Grey use | Honest twin |
|---|---|---|
| **Assumptive** (“When do we deliver?”) | Treats no as yes | After they already opened Chat: “Paste your key.” One next step, not a pretend sale |
| **Alternative / which** | Hides “walk away” | Free-with-banner **or** $4.99 once. Walking away is also fine |
| **Puppy-dog** (leave the vacuum) | Guilt when they like it | They can send messages for free. Pay is later, optional |
| **Impending event** (“price goes up Monday”) | Lie about Monday | Only if the price is really changing. We have no reason to |
| **Fear close** (“your kids will suffer”) | Invented harm | Real harm only: this phone may drop extras. Said once, calmly |
| **Sharp-angle** (“if I can, will you buy?”) | Boxes them | Never. We do not haggle |
| **Take-away** (“maybe this isn’t for you”) | Insult | If the phone is too weak for an extra, we turn the extra **off**. That is care, not a dare |

## What we actually put in the product (bones, not a poster)

These are the good twins as **behavior**. Users never hear the history.

1. Opens. They type. They get an answer. That is the demo.
2. Key stays on the phone. No account wall.
3. Memory note is true and boring.
4. One banner, or $4.99 once, shown before Pay.
5. Decline / Not now is a normal button.
6. We do not grow a new personality to squeeze a sale.

That is the used Honda. It starts. It does not yell.

- Hard cancel, silent subscription, drip fees

## Say it like I'm five

People love a story where a kid ships an app on Tuesday and owns the world on Friday.

Almost none of those stories are true.

What looks like one night is usually:

1. Years of boring work nobody clapped for.
2. A product that does **one** thing well.
3. One lucky door (a streamer, a footer, a free preview).
4. The team was **still around** when the door opened.

You cannot plant lightning. You can be standing in the field with a working radio when the sky cracks.

This file is the radio. Not a rain dance.

## Story by story (only what we need)

### 1. Angry Birds — eight years, then a slingshot

Rovio (first called Relude) started in 2003 from a Finnish demo party. They made dozens of games. In 2009 they were near bankruptcy. *Angry Birds* was game **52**, built in eight months. Downloads then shot up “in a matter of weeks.”[2]

Wired later said the “overnight” took eight years, and in English-speaking stores it was a flop for the first three months.[3] Chris Dixon used the same 52-game fact as the poster child of the myth.[10]

**Lesson:** the hit was one simple loop (pull, fling, giggle). Not a new personality every week.

**Our twin:** keep the same small chat app. Make *honesty* stronger (size number → memory note → receipt). Do not become a 52nd gimmick factory.

### 2. Among Us — two years of almost nobody

Released June 2018. Almost wrapped as “done.” Korea found it after an itch.io front page (Dec 2018). Brazil kept it alive. Steam Daily Deal. Then 2020 streamers. A marketer who dug the logs: **this will not happen to your game.** Don’t structure the business on a shooting star.[9]

**Lesson:** they were still shipping, still playable, still findable.

**Our twin:** stay installed. Stay small. Stay honest. If someone films a cheap phone chatting, we did not pay them to lie.

### 3. Flappy Bird — sleeper, then a panic

Dong Nguyen built it in two or three days. iOS: May 24, 2013. Quiet. Spike after a PewDiePie review. #1 free iOS game end of January 2014. Android: January 30, 2014. He said ~$50,000 a day from ads. He **removed it** February 10, 2014. People sold phones that still had it.[1]

**Lesson:** tiny + one tap can explode. Fame can also break the maker.

**Our twin:** we want boring reliability, not a guilt crash. If it ever spikes, **do not take it down.** Have a one-page “how to get a key” ready. No fake “limited copies.”

### 4. Wordle — a grid you can share without spoiling

Josh Wardle made it for him and his partner. October public. Millions. Sold to the New York Times for a low seven-figure sum. He said it got bigger than he imagined and was overwhelming. NYT said it would “initially remain free,” which made fans nervous about a later wall.[6]

**Lesson:** the **share object** was the colored squares, not a trailer. One thing a day. No account.

**Our twin:** the share object is a **true picture**: the app open, a reply streaming, maybe a size or “low memory” note. Not a boxing poster. Not a fake score.

### 5. Hotmail — the product *was* the flyer

Launch July 4, 1996. 100 signups in the first hour by word of mouth. Investor Tim Draper pushed a footer on every outbound mail. Founders hated it (felt like pollution). They added the signup line (dropped “P.S. I love you”). Then ~3,000 users a day, 1 million in six months, 2 million five weeks later. India: one mail, then 100,000 users in three weeks. Juno spent $20 million on ads and still lost.[7]

**Lesson:** using it *showed* a friend that it worked and it was free.

**Grey warning:** they stamped ads on other people’s letters. Our good-twin law forbids that trap.

**Our twin:** if a friend sees you chatting on a cheap phone, that is the footer. If we ever add “share,” it is a **tap they choose**, not a stamp we hide.

### 6. ChatGPT — seven years, then a free box

OpenAI spent seven years on language models (GPT-3 in 2020). ChatGPT preview: November 30, 2022. 1 million users in five days (Altman). UBS: 100 million monthly users by January 2023 — faster than TikTok and Instagram to that mark.[4][5]

Then they charged $20/month. That rent is **our villain product**, not a person. H-006: we do not yell that on the store.

**Lesson:** the overnight was a **free, stupid-simple window** on years of hidden work.

**Our twin:** we will never be ChatGPT. We are the small window that uses *their* key. Their boom is our weather. We do not pretend we trained the model.

### 7. Opera Mini — the anti-meme

No one talks about Opera Mini as an overnight. They just… owned weak phones and expensive data until 300 million users (Feb 2013, all Opera products).[8]

**Lesson:** the empty slot on a cheap phone is a **decade job**, not a week job.

**Our twin:** this is the closest cousin. Stay the small AI chat that actually opens.

## What “overnight” really is (five machines)

1. **Sleeper** — already shipped. Time passes. Then a door. (Flappy, Among Us)
2. **52nd try** — same shop, simpler loop. (Angry Birds)
3. **Share object** — people can post proof without a speech. (Wordle grid)
4. **Product-is-the-ad** — using it shows a friend it works. (Hotmail, with the trap removed)
5. **Free window on old work** — years in the lab, one boring box. (ChatGPT)

None of these need a fake countdown. Grey-sales file still wins.

# The roadmap (walk this)

Chapters, not “v1.0.” Everyday face. Fight stays in the bones.

### Chapter 0 — Stop waiting for lightning

Accept Among Us rule #1: you cannot plant it.[9]  
If it happens, it is a gift. The business is **the Honda that starts**.

**Done when:** this file is graded by PROOF. No Ready child from this chapter.

### Chapter 1 — The demo *is* the product

Kirby dumped dirt on their floor. Hotmail showed the inbox. ChatGPT was a box you typed in.

**Do:**

- First open → paste key → a real answer. Under a minute.
- No account wall.
- One calm memory note if the phone is weak.
- Free chat is really free. $4.99 once, shown before Pay.

**Don’t:** eleven feature bullets. A “coming soon” wall.

**$:** $0. This is code you already want.

### Chapter 2 — One shareable proof (Wordle squares)

People need something they can show a cousin **without a speech**.

**Do:**

- A real screenshot: chat working on a normal/cheap phone.
- Optional: the true APK size or a true “low memory” screen.
- If they share, they chose to.

**Don’t:** fake “2,481 people bought today.” Fake clocks. Bout titles.

**$:** $0 phone + free screen recorder.

### Chapter 3 — Underground week, then Play

Same as H-005 (still your call). F-Droid / XDA / 4PDA *before* Play is just shipping order, like itch.io before Steam.[9]

**Do:** let the first users be people who already hunt small APKs.

**Don’t:** call it a secret fight card.

**$:** Play listing later ~$25 one-time (flag again when you tap pay). Play Billing later takes a cut of $4.99.

### Chapter 4 — Stay the 52nd game, not game 53–80

Angry Birds won because they finally made **one** simple thing.[2][3]

**Do:** same face for years (`applicationId` freezes after first Play upload — already in historic sales doc).

**Don’t:** weekly gimmicks, on-device 7B, agent runtime, WebView bloat.

### Chapter 5 — Own the Opera Mini slot

Be findable as “the small one that works on this phone” **without saying underdog**.

**Do:** keep RSS/APK laws. Post real failures if a phone can’t run it.

**Don’t:** “built for hackers.” Name test still: grandma hears “I put my key in and chat.”

### Chapter 6 — If lightning hits

Have a one-pager ready (docs, not a new personality):

- How to get an API key (their money, not ours).
- What $4.99 does (kills the banner).
- What we will not do (no 7B, no surprise bill).

**Don’t:** pull the listing. Don’t raise price with a fake “ends Monday.”

### Chapter 7 — Aftercatch only after a real message

Shareware: they already sent a chat. Then Pro. Grey-sales file.

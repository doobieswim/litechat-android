## Say it like I'm five

A fighter does not get famous by being good in the gym.

A fighter gets famous because someone **builds a story around him**, then **puts that story where people already stand**, then **makes them pick a side**.

The product is the punch.
The shine is the story around the punch.

LiteChat already has a punch: thin BYOK chat that can say “your phone is too weak.”
It does not yet have a **fight**.

## The old promoters, in baby words

### 1. John L. Sullivan — “I can lick any man in the house”

Sullivan was the first boxing superstar. He did not wait for a title belt. He walked into bars and said he could beat anyone there. Then he put that dare on a train.[6]

In 1883–84 he rode coast to coast. 195 fights. 136 towns. 238 days. The dare: he would box **anyone, anytime**, Queensberry rules, for **$250**. He knocked out eleven men on that tour.[6]

His last big bare-knuckle title fight (Sullivan vs Kilrain, 1889) was **illegal in every state**. So they hid it. Tickets from New Orleans said only “From New Orleans to Destination.” Two thousand people got on a train to a secret field in Mississippi. 75 rounds. 104°F. National newspapers ate it.[10]

**The brain trick:** a public dare is better than an ad. A secret destination is better than a press release. People tell each other.

**Steal (honest):**
- Play / Reddit / XDA dare: “I can run on any 4GB phone in the house.” Not “we are lightweight.” A **$0 open challenge**. Anyone posts a device. We either run or we admit we lost.
- Underground first: F-Droid / 4PDA / XDA *before* Play, like the secret Mississippi field. The people who ride that train become the story.

**Do not steal:** Sullivan’s “color line” (he refused Black fighters). That is poison. We do not gate who may take the dare.

### 2. Tex Rickard — the fight is not the product

Rickard was a gambler who stumbled into boxing to sell a mining town. Goldfield, Nevada, 1906: Gans vs Nelson. He put **$30,000 in gold coins in a store window** so people could *see* the purse. Gate: over $62,000. Lesson he kept forever: **the fight is only part of the product. The spectacle around it is where the money lives.**[1][3]

1910, Johnson vs Jeffries in Reno. He did not hide the hate. He sold the hate. Johnson won. Riots followed. Rickard learned: **controversy, handled, sells more than love.**[1]

1921, Dempsey vs Carpentier. First million-dollar gate. ~90,000 people. First live radio title fight (~300,000 listeners). He built a wooden stadium just for the night. He did not sell “two men punch.” He sold **hero vs slacker**. Carpentier = French war hero, pretty, “the Orchid Man.” Dempsey = accused draft dodger, barrooms, brute. Good vs evil. The fight itself was boring. The *story* filled the papers the next day.[1][4]

Frank Deford later said Rickard “first recognized the potential of the star system.”[3]

**The brain trick:** people do not buy a sport. They buy a **moral play**. Polarization beats affection.

**Steal (honest):**
- Gold coins in the window = **public numbers**. APK size. Idle RSS. “We told this phone no.” Screenshot the matrix. Do not say “tiny.” Show the coins.
- Polarize: fat official ChatGPT / fat Agora vs the thin honest one. Not “we are nicer.” **Hero vs rent-collector.** Their sin is real (monthly bill, 50MB APK, “runs on any phone” lies).
- Build the arena: one launch *event* with a date, not a quiet Play upload. A night. A name. A radio (a live thread).

**Do not steal:** selling race hate. Selling a fake draft-dodger smear. Polarize on **product sins**, not people.

### 3. Doc Kearns — the hustler who made the gate

Kearns managed Dempsey. Hall of Fame calls him “a master of publicity” and credits him with making the first million-dollar gate possible. He also got Dempsey $300,000 for a fight in **Shelby, Montana that nearly bankrupted the town.** The *LA Times* later called him one of the great sports con men of the century.[8][9][14]

He did not only sell fights. He sold film, vaudeville, circus tours, personal appearances — the fighter as a traveling show.[14]

**The brain trick:** the boxer is a **character who tours**. The bout is one stop.

**Steal (honest):**
- Tour the *forums*, not the towns. One week 4PDA. One week r/androidafterlife. One week a cheap-phone Discord. Same dare every stop.
- Side shows: APK weigh-in video. Compat-matrix “weigh-in.” Cost-per-message receipt. These are Kearns’s vaudeville — extra stages, same star.

**Do not steal:** bankrupting a partner. Cooking the books. Kearns and Dempsey ended in lawsuits. If we ever take money from users, the books stay boring and public.

### 4. Don King — name the night, move the night

King’s face *was* the ad. Hair, shout, “Only in America!” He did not say “Ali vs Foreman, Kinshasa, 1974.” He said **The Rumble in the Jungle**. Then **The Thrilla in Manila**.[2][7]

He signed both men to $5 million each **before he had the money**, then found a country that would pay to host. Mobutu wanted the spotlight. The fight became a country-sized poster. Maybe a billion TV viewers. Ali was a 4–1 underdog. He won by rope-a-dope — let the big man punch himself tired, then knock him out.[2][7]

Many fighters later sued King for stealing from them. Mike Tyson: “He did more bad to black fighters than any white promoter ever.”[2]

**The brain trick:** a **named night** is a product. A weird place is a story. An underdog script is a ticket.

**Steal (honest):**
- Name the launch like a bout, not a version number. Not “v1.0.” Something like “The Weigh-In” or “4GB vs the World.” (Final name waits on H-004.)
- Take it somewhere unexpected: a used-phone shop video. A 2016 Moto G. A cybercafé. Not a Pixel 9 review unit.
- Rope-a-dope as product: we do not out-punch Agora on features. We **lean on the ropes** (stay thin) and let fat apps tire the phone. Then we look fast.

**Do not steal:** sign a purse you cannot pay. Steal from the fighter (the user). King’s crimes are the anti-pattern. Our Pro is $4.99 once, on the receipt, no club.

### 5. Muhammad Ali — the weigh-in is the show

Before Ali, a weigh-in was a scale. In 1964 Cassius Clay got in Sonny Liston’s face. The prefight circus was born.[13]

He cast Liston as the dragon and himself as the pretty hero who would slay it: “He’s too ugly to be world champion. The world champ should be pretty like me.” He was a huge underdog. He won.[12]

The talk sold the fight **and** got inside the other guy’s head. “I had to talk him down before I could knock him down.”[unverified] (widely quoted; treat as folklore unless we find the primary tape.)

**The brain trick:** rhyme + insult + underdog = free ink. People come to see if the loud kid dies.

**Steal (honest, kind):**
- Trash-talk **apps**, not people. “50 megabytes to say hello is ugly.” “A $20/month chatbot is too ugly to be champ.”
- A public weigh-in: film our APK on a kitchen scale metaphor — `ls -lh` vs Agora. Post the numbers. Invite them to step on the scale.
- Poetry that a cheap-phone owner can repeat. One couplet. Not a manifesto.

**Do not steal:** calling a *person* ugly. Ali’s Frazier “Uncle Tom” line hurt a man for life. We do not do that.

## Carnival and circus — the midway is a Play Store

Fight guys stole from carnival guys. Carnival guys stole from Barnum.

A sideshow talker (never “barker”) stands on a **bally** platform. He gives a **free taste**. A sword, a flash, a girl turning into a gorilla for three seconds. That builds a **tip** (a crowd). Then he sends them inside to pay. Inside, after each act, an **aftercatch**: buy the photo, the tiny bible, the trick envelope.[5]

Barnum put the Feejee Mermaid in a case (a monkey sewn to a fish) and *loved* being called a fake. Accusations sold more tickets. “Persons who pay their money at the door have a right to form their own opinions.”[5]

He also had Joice Heth “Washington’s 161-year-old nurse” — an old woman sold as a relic. That is a **gaff**. A lie with a ticket price.[5]

**The brain trick:** free sample outside. Paid wonder inside. A banner bigger than the truth. A fight about “is it real?” that *is* the ad.

**Steal (honest):**
- Bally = Play screenshots + a 15-second GIF of the compat matrix turning a row **red**. The red cell is the free taste. “We told this phone no.”
- Aftercatch = $4.99 Pro after they already chat. Never before.
- Banner line = one painted claim. Not ten feature icons.

**Do not steal:** gaffs. SoftRAM was a gaff. “Runs 7B on 4GB” is a gaff. If someone accuses us of being small, we agree. We do not sew a mermaid.

## One more aisle: the Pepsi Challenge (1975)

Pepsi put two white cups in a mall. Blind sip. People picked Pepsi more often. Then the cameras showed the surprise. Coke still won the war of *identity*, but Pepsi won the war of **proof in public**.[11]

**Steal:** a **RAM Challenge**, not a taste test.

Same cheap phone.
Same prompt.
Our app vs the fat one.
Timer + “did it die?”

Film it. Do not fake it. If we lose, we post the loss. That is the anti-gaff. Barnum would hate it. Hopkins would love it.

**Cost:** $0 if you already own the phones. **PAID** if you buy devices or run mall booths or buy ads around the video.

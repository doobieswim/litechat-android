## Say it like I'm five

There are three ways to put on a fight show.

1. **WWE / American cartoon.** Costumes. Catchphrases. Fireworks. The story happens on the microphone.
2. **Strong Style (New Japan / Inoki).** Pretend it is a real martial art. Hard kicks. Submissions. “This is a sport.”
3. **King's Road (All Japan / Giant Baba).** Almost no costume talk. The story happens **with the body**. You get knocked down. You get up. Next year the same move does not put you away. The crowd cries when the young one finally pins the old king.

You do not want fireworks.
You do not want to pretend we are ChatGPT.
You want **King's Road**.

The old well-off apps are Jumbo Tsuruta and Stan Hansen: big, paid, already champ.
We are Misawa the day he took the mask off: smaller, poorer, and the building is already chanting.

## What King's Road actually is

Giant Baba founded All Japan Pro Wrestling in 1972. The company later listed its style as **King's Road**.[6][7]

The Japanese word is **ōdō** (王道) — “the royal road,” the proper way.[2]

Baba and his tag partner Antonio Inoki split. Inoki built New Japan and **Strong Style** (wrestling as martial art). Baba built All Japan and **King's Road** (wrestling as a long story told with stamina).[1][9]

King's Road is not “more punches.” It is a **booking language**:

- The drama happens **in the ring**, not in a promo. No giant costume. No cartoon catchphrase.[1]
- Baba planned careers **months and years** ahead. Wins and losses *meant* something. A rookie did not beat a veteran by accident.[1]
- A match has a shape: feel each other out → work a weakness → reverse → **fighting spirit** rises → one man has enough spirit to hit the finisher.[1]
- The same two men meet again and again. The first time, the vet puts the kid away with **one** finisher. The next time it takes **two**. Later the old finisher is “just another move.” The vet must invent a **stronger** version. If the kid kicks out of three finishers and still stands, the crowd treats that as a kind of win even when he loses.[1]
- The old formula: smaller Japanese wrestler eventually beats the bigger foreigner (*gaijin*) through spirit, not size.[1]
- 1990s peak: four men of the same generation — **Misawa, Kawada, Kobashi, Taue** — the Four Pillars / Four Heavenly Kings. Their matches *are* the style.[2][4]

Puroresu in general treats “fighting spirit” and getting up when you should stay down as the point of the show.[5]

King's Road vs Strong Style in one line:[8][9]

| | Strong Style | King's Road |
|---|---|---|
| Pretends to be | A real fight / MMA cousin | A long story |
| Wins with | Same submission, again | A *new, stronger* version of the move |
| Cares about | Looking legitimate | Memory. Last year. Next year. |
| Danger | Getting hurt to look real | Getting hurt to raise the story |

**We steal the grammar. We do not steal the injuries.** Mitsuharu Misawa died in the ring in 2009 after years of that style. Wikipedia calls the physical cost of ōdō “problematic.”[2] For an app that means: do not add features until we break. Rope-a-dope still stands. Stay thin. Kick out. Do not take the backdrop.

## The one night that is our story

May 14, 1990: Misawa is still **Tiger Mask II**. The wins belong to the mask, not the man. He is getting beaten up. He tells his partner to pull the mask off. The crowd chants **his real name**. He starts winning.[3]

June 8, 1990: less than a month later, he faces Jumbo Tsuruta — the old Ace. Baba had planned for the kid to lose a “valiant effort.” Then Baba walked past the merch tables, saw how badly the fans wanted Misawa, and **changed the finish**. Misawa pins Jumbo. People cry. Tokyo shows sell out for years.[2][3]

That is the whole theme:

1. Take the mask off (stop hiding behind a generic AI-chat name).
2. Fight the old champ in public.
3. Let the crowd decide if the kid is ready — do not fake a win we did not earn.

## The match shape = the product and the listing

King's Road match, mapped to one install:

**1. Feel-out (first 10 seconds)**
Do not dump features. Light contact. One screenshot. One sentence. “This is a small fighter. The big apps are fatter than your phone.”

**2. Work the weakness**
Their knee is RAM theater and monthly rent. The compat matrix is the chop to that knee. The red cell is a kick they cannot sell as “fine.”

**3. They hit their finishers**
- Splash: huge APK. We kick out (size number in the window).
- Lariat: $20/month. We kick out ($4.99 once, or free with one banner).
- Superplex: “runs any model / agent on device.” We kick out by **refusing** (Tier A only). Refusing is fighting spirit, not a missing feature.

**4. Fighting spirit spot**
On a TIGHT phone the UI should feel like getting up: stream still paints, Stop still works, we do not OOM-pose. If we die, we post the death. A King's Road loss that forces their strongest move is respect.

**5. The new finisher (ours)**
Do not invent a new gimmick every update. Invent a **stronger version of honesty**. v1: red cell. v2: “this message cost $0.0003.” v3: public RAM Challenge video. Same move. Higher tier. That is ōdō, not a WWE new-entrance.

**6. Time**
Baba booked years. We do not need years of vapor. We need **chapters**:
- Chapter 1: unmask (rename).
- Chapter 2: first public weigh-in (APK vs Agora).
- Chapter 3: first time a user phone we were supposed to lose on… doesn't.
- Chapter 4: they copy the red cell. That is them using their *strongest* move. We already won the respect.

## Theme rules (what “looks like King's Road” vs what does not)

**Looks like King's Road**
- Dark, small, a little tired, still standing.
- Numbers on the poster (weight class = APK / RAM band).
- Clean finish. No “download now and maybe it works.”
- A loss we publish.
- One rival at a time in the copy. Not “better than everyone.”
- Long memory: “last year they needed 40MB; this year we still fit.”

**Looks like WWE (do not)**
- Neon “AI UNLEASHED” launch.
- Eleven feature icons.
- A mascot in sunglasses.
- “The most powerful chat on Android.”

**Looks like Strong Style (do not)**
- Pretending we are a local LLM.
- “Real AI on your phone” when the brain is their key.
- Fake sport legitimacy. We are a thin window. Say that.

**Looks like a gaff / SoftRAM (never)**
- Sewing a mermaid. “Runs 7B on 4GB.”
